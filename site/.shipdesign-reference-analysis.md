# Reference Translation

## Short analysis

The reference uses an editorial industrial system: a pale technical hero interrupted by a high-chroma utility rail, large display headline, sparse navigation, then alternating dark and light bands. Sections are long and modular, with one dominant statement, one proof or workflow object, and generous negative space. Oversized display type pairs with compact technical labels. Thin grid lines, diagram overlays, and small arrow controls create an instrument-panel feel.

## Transfer

- Alternate light and dark surfaces for narrative cadence.
- Use an acid utility rail or status spine as a recurring orientation cue.
- Give each section one dominant statement and one visual proof object.
- Pair oversized editorial headings with compact technical metadata.
- Use technical rules, grid overlays, outlined route diagrams, and small directional controls.
- Vary macro compositions across hero, statement, workflow, sources, principles, install, and close.

## Do not copy

- Trackwell branding, logo, yellow and black identity, warehouse copy, robotics imagery, or exact illustrations.
- The reference navigation, section order, FAQ/footer composition, and source-specific numbers or claims.
- The reference serif face as a literal asset or any visual treatment that makes ShipDesign look like a logistics company.

## Adapted direction

**ShipDesign Control Room:** an editorial orchestration site where acid green is the active system signal, paper and ink bands form the narrative, and the seven-source workflow becomes an annotated routing instrument. The existing command-stage hero remains the product anchor; the rest of the page gains alternating cadence, technical rails, and proof-led composition.
